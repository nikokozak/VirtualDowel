Thick stock with half slots

How to set up the slots and bays in Fusion so that Virtual Dowel can find side B on stock too thick to drill through, and what the probe does at each slot.

1. Why half slots

The hole mode needs a reference hole through the whole board. The cutter for that hole must start above the board, so the Z travel must take the board, a cutter as long as the board is thick, and the collet: about 2 to 2.5 times the thickness. Thick stock does not fit.

A half slot is a reference hole moved out to the stock edge, so it opens to the outside. Side A cuts it from face A to a little past mid-thickness, from outside the board. On side B, a bay cut from face B opens a path down to it. The probe then comes in from the side and probes the slot as it probes a hole. Each side cuts only about half the thickness, the same as the flip-milling job itself needs. So the half-slot mode works wherever the flip-milling job works.

2. What Virtual Dowel does and does not do

Virtual Dowel probes, checks and aligns your files. It never makes toolpaths. You design the slots, the bays and the side-B job in Fusion, or in any CAM that writes ShopBot OpenSBP files. Virtual Dowel then:

  • gives you the minimum sizes for the slots and bays, from your probe and stock (section 5);
  • rotates and shifts your bay file roughly, from where you jog to the slot openings (section 8);
  • probes the slots and rotates and shifts your side-B job files exactly, as the hole mode does.

3. Side A in Fusion

Side A is placed as in the hole mode: zero X and Y at the corner of the Setup 1 WCS and run the job. Side A is never probed.

Rule for the side-A slotsWhy
Two slots on the flip axis, one at each end, opening to the outside.On the flip axis the side-A and side-B coordinates are the same. Far apart gives the best rotation.
Different widths, at least 0.1 in apart.The widths tell the slots apart, so a board flipped the wrong way fails.
Cut with an end mill that moves inward from outside the stock to a round end.The cutter never has to be above the stock. The centre of the round end is the slot's reference point: enter its X and Y in Virtual Dowel.
Depth from face A: as deep as the side-A tool allows, past mid-thickness.The bay on side B must reach it.
Width and length at least as the size calculator says. Make the slot longer by how far your side-A zero may be off.The probe needs room on a turned board and must touch the straight part of the walls.
Zero within about 0.1 in along the flip axis, or cut the key notch.The rough fit takes each slot opening to be at the stock-box edge (section 8).

4. Side B in Fusion: the bays

Set up Setup 2 as for the hole mode. Then add the bays: one above each slot, cut from face B. Put them in their own posted file (the bay file), so that Virtual Dowel can place it before the slots can be probed.

Rule for the baysWhy
Open to the outside edge.The probe cannot go above the stock, so it must come in from the side. A closed pocket, round or not, cannot be reached.
Enter from outside the stock. No plunge or ramp into material.Every plunge happens in the air.
Depth: 0.15 in into the top of the side-A slot.The bay and the slot must meet.
Width and reach at least as the size calculator says, measured from the slot's round-end centre.The probe body follows the ball. If the body is below the stock top while probing, the bay must fit it beside every wall touch and at the round end, plus 0.4 in for the rough fit.
Bays in waste only. Cutter cutting length at least the bay depth + 0.1 in.The bay file has no part geometry.

5. Sizes

The size calculator gives the minimum slot and bay sizes and the park height for your probe and stock. It reads nothing from your files. In a command window in the VirtualDowel folder:

py -3 vd_align.py sizes --ball 0.125 --stem 0.08 --free 1 --body 0.8 --length 3 ^
   --thick 3 --slot-depth 1.75 --widths 0.625,0.75 --slot-length 1.25 ^
   --bay-width 2.3 --bay-reach 1.2

It prints OK, or NOT OK with the problems. Add --mm to give every value in millimetres. The inputs:

6. At the machine

First time? Do the dry run and the first machine tests in the README, section 3b, before a real part.

  1. Side AClamp the stock. Zero X and Y at the corner of the Setup 1 WCS, within about 0.1 in along the flip axis. Zero Z. Run side A, slots included.
  2. FlipFlip the board over the flip axis and clamp it anywhere, turned up to 5°. Keep the slot openings and the space for the bays free of clamps.
  3. Bay cutterFit the bay cutter. Do not zero X or Y on side B, now or later.
  4. Rough locateType C80. Under Locate side B with choose Half slots, add the side-B files and the bay file, enter the slots and the stock box, click Check, then Start rough locate (bay cutter in). For each slot, in the order the window shows, jog the bit tip over the slot opening on the edge face and confirm. The key notch comes last, if you cut one.
  5. Bay cutThe window places the bay file as <bay file>_VDROUGH.sbp. Look at it in Preview. Zero Z, then run it.
  6. CleanVacuum the slots and bays. A chip on a wall moves a touch.
  7. ProbeFit the probe and plug in its cable.
  8. Probe the slotsType C80 and click Start probing (probe in). For each slot, jog the probe ball to outside the opening, anywhere up to 1 in out, in line with the slot, inside the park height band, and confirm. The routine does the rest (section 7).
  9. AlignThe window aligns the side-B files: PASS writes <job>_VD.sbp.
  10. Cut side BFit each cutter, zero Z only, and run each _VD.sbp.

7. What the probe does at each slot

Every move below the stock top is guarded: the probe input stops it. Each error stop backs the probe out of the slot the way it came in.

  1. Move 1.2 in into the opening from wherever you parked. In a short slot the round end is touched on the way: then the probe backs off by the slot's half width plus 0.08 in, which puts it on the straight part. A touch on the edge face, because the park was off-line, is caught at the next step.
  2. Look for the two slot walls across the slot and centre on them. The first search is short, so that in a bay the probe body cannot reach the bay side; the search for the other wall crosses the whole width. No walls means the ball is too high, in the bay, off-line, or parked more than an inch out. This check comes before the long search, so that the probe body can never reach the end of the bay.
  3. Search along the slot for the round end.
  4. Back off by the half width plus 0.08 in: the wall touches land 0.08 in behind the round end, whatever the park distance was.
  5. One coarse and then the fine passes: wall, wall, end. Then back out to the park point.

With spin-180 on, the routine asks you to turn the spindle by hand after each slot and probes it again. That cancels stylus runout.

8. How the bay file is placed

The bays must be cut before the slots can be probed, so the bay file is placed by a rough fit. Each slot opening shows on the edge face, and it is where the slot axis meets the stock-box edge. Two openings jogged by eye give the rotation and both shifts.

  • Accuracy: in the simulator, with a 0.1 in jog error and a board turned 5°, the bays land within 0.24 in when the real edge is within 0.15 in of the stock box. That includes your side-A zero error along the flip axis.
  • Irregular stock: where the real edge is not near the stock box, an opening only shows where the slot axis is, not where along it the slot is. Cut the key notch in an edge at right angles to the flip axis and jog to it last; it gives the missing position. It gets no bay and is never probed.
  • Never a part file: the output is named _VDROUGH, a file with cuts more than 3 in from the slots is refused, and the probing that follows deletes the rough file.

11. Through slots

If you have a bit as long as the stock is thick, but not the Z travel to get it above the stock, cut the slots through the full thickness from outside. No bays and no rough locate: on side B, go straight to probing (step 7 of section 6). The retract and linking moves of that bit must stay low and outside the stock.

12. Accuracy

  • Across a slot, the two wall touches go in opposite directions, so ball size, trigger travel, stop latency and backlash cancel, as in a hole.
  • Along a slot, the end touch goes one way only. A push past contact that is the same on both axes cancels against the wall pair. What remains, for example backlash, has the opposite sign at the two opposite slots, so it cancels in the position and shows only as a spacing error in the report.
  • Stylus runout shifts every touch the same way: use spin-180, or keep runout under 0.0005 in.
  • Expected registration: about that of the hole mode, 0.001 to 0.002 in. This is an estimate from the simulator, not yet measured on the machine.
